**ChatGPT can tidy a performance review you already wrote in your head. It cannot judge the person. Work in five steps: gather dated examples, restate the role expectations, strip names and pay data, ask for a structured draft, then check every line for unsupported praise, unfair criticism, and bias you did not intend.**

## What ChatGPT is actually good for in a review

**The model is good at structure, tone, and consistency. It can turn eleven scattered notes into three readable paragraphs, soften a blunt sentence without hiding the point, and keep six reviews in the same format. It is not good at deciding whether someone met expectations, because it has never seen a minute of the work.**

Most managers do not get stuck on the verdict. They get stuck on the writing. You know that one person carried the fall rollout and that another keeps missing handoffs, but turning that into paragraphs that sound fair, specific, and calm takes an hour you do not have.

That is the gap worth closing. The judgment stays with you. The sentence construction is the part you can hand over.

The risk sits in the same place as the benefit. A language model will happily write a confident paragraph about someone it knows nothing about. Give it three thin notes and it will produce a polished page, and the polish makes weak evidence feel stronger than it is. That is the single failure mode to watch for the whole way through.

## The five-step evidence-to-draft workflow

**Follow the order. Evidence first, expectations second, redaction third, draft fourth, your own review last. Skipping the first two steps is what produces vague filler like "consistently demonstrates strong ownership." Skipping the last step is what puts a sentence in front of an employee that you cannot defend out loud.**

1. **Collect dated examples.** Open your notes, your ticket system, your sent mail. Write a flat list: date, what happened, what the impact was. Six to twelve items covering the whole period, not just the last month. If you cannot find evidence for a criticism, that criticism does not go in the review.
2. **Write the role expectations in your own words.** Three to six bullets on what this role is supposed to deliver. Without this, the model invents a generic standard, and generic standards are how reviews drift into unfairness.
3. **Redact before you paste.** Remove full names, employee IDs, salary, bonus figures, health details, and anything about a complaint or investigation. Use "the coordinator" or "Employee A." See the table below for the line by line version.
4. **Ask for a structured draft.** Tell it the sections you want, the length, and the tone. Ask it to cite which of your listed examples supports each claim. That one instruction makes padding easy to spot.
5. **Own every sentence.** Read the draft against your evidence list. Delete anything you would not say to the person directly. Rewrite at least a few lines in your own voice so it sounds like you and not like a template.

## A safe practice run with an invented employee

**Do the first attempt on a person who does not exist. Invent a scheduling coordinator, write six fake incidents, and run the whole workflow. You get to see how the model behaves, including where it overstates, without any real employee's record touching the chat window.**

Here is an illustrative example, made up for practice, not drawn from any real company:

> Role: scheduling coordinator, six months in seat.
> Expectations: same day patient callbacks, accurate calendar, clean handoff notes to billing.
> Evidence: Mar 4, rebuilt the double booking rules, no conflicts since. Apr 18, three callbacks missed during a staff absence. May 2, trained two new hires without being asked. Jun 9, handoff notes to billing incomplete twice in one week.

Then a prompt like this:

```
You are helping me draft a performance review. Use only the evidence below.
Do not add achievements or concerns that are not listed.
Write three sections: strengths, areas to develop, goals for next quarter.
Under each claim, name which piece of evidence supports it.
Neutral, specific tone. Around 250 words.
[paste role, expectations, evidence]
```

Watch what comes back. If it praises "excellent communication" and cannot point to a listed item, that is the model filling space. Deleting those lines is the skill this exercise teaches.

## What to strip, and what to check afterward

**Two passes protect you. The first removes information that should never sit in a chat window. The second reads the finished draft for claims your evidence does not carry. Both are quick, and both are the difference between a helpful tool and an HR problem you created yourself.**

| In your notes | Paste it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dated work examples and outcomes | Yes | This is the evidence the draft has to rest on |
| Role expectations and team goals | Yes | Gives the model a standard instead of inventing one |
| Full names and employee IDs | No | Use "the coordinator" or "Employee A" instead |
| Salary, bonus, raise figures | No | Compensation belongs in your HR system, not a chat |
| Medical, leave, or accommodation details | No | Sensitive category, and irrelevant to performance writing |
| Complaints, investigations, legal matters | No | Handle through HR and counsel, never through a draft tool |
| Peer feedback with the peer's name attached | No | Strip attribution, keep the substance if it is verified |

The same in and out logic applies across sensitive work generally. [ChatGPT for accounting: what goes in, what never does](/articles/chatgpt-for-accounting) covers the financial version of this line, and [Is ChatGPT Confidential? A Plain-English Answer](/articles/is-chatgpt-confidential) explains what actually happens to text you type into a consumer account, which is worth ten minutes before you use this at work.

Your check pass, once the draft exists:

- Every positive claim traces to a dated example. If not, cut it.
- Every criticism traces to a dated example. If not, cut it.
- No personality words standing in for behavior. "Abrasive" is a judgment. "Interrupted twice in the Apr 18 handoff meeting" is an observation.
- The whole period is represented, not only the last three weeks.
- Compare your drafts across the team. If the quiet people all got shorter reviews, that is a bias signal in your notes, not in the model.
- Read the final version out loud. Anything you would not say to the person's face comes out.

## When to skip this entirely

**Do not use ChatGPT for a review tied to termination, a formal improvement plan, a demotion, or anything connected to a complaint or investigation. Those documents can end up in a legal proceeding, and their wording needs your HR team and possibly counsel, not a drafting assistant. Check your employer's AI policy before you start.**

There is one more honest limit. If your notes are thin, this workflow will show you that fast, and no prompt fixes it. A review built on two remembered incidents is a weak review whether you wrote it yourself or not. The real repair is keeping a short running log through the year, four lines a month per person, so next cycle has something to stand on.

Small teams sometimes find that the better fix is not an AI draft at all but a habit change in how observations get recorded. That question is worth asking before you reach for a tool.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it wrong to use AI for a performance review at all?

Using it to write clearly is reasonable. Using it to decide the rating is not. The distinction that matters is whether every claim came from your observation. If you can defend each sentence with a date and an example, the drafting help is a formatting convenience, not a delegation of judgment.

### Should I tell my employee that I used ChatGPT?

Check your company policy first, since some now require disclosure. Beyond policy, most people care less about the tool and more about whether the content is accurate and fair. If your review is built on real dated examples, you can answer the question honestly and the conversation moves on.

### What if the draft sounds nothing like me?

That is normal and easy to fix. Rewrite the opening and closing lines yourself, keep the middle structure, and replace any phrase you would never say. Two or three edits usually restore your voice. A review that sounds like a template reads as low effort no matter how it was produced.

### Will ChatGPT make my reviews more consistent across the team?

It can make the format consistent, which helps. It cannot make your underlying judgment consistent, because the model only sees what you feed it. If you give richer notes for one person than another, the drafts will reflect that gap faithfully. Consistency starts in your note keeping.

### How long should this take once I am used to it?

Gathering evidence is the slow part, usually twenty to thirty minutes per person if your notes are scattered. The drafting and checking afterward runs closer to ten. If the whole thing takes over an hour, the problem is almost always missing notes rather than the tool.
